   Hardness of Coloring with Seven Connected Bundles

                              Working manuscript · awaiting expert assessment
                                            16 September 2026


Abstract
We give a deterministic polynomial-time reduction from bipartite list coloring with a global palette
of three colors to weighted coloring with seven connected bundles. Six bundles are singletons; the
remaining bundle contains the source graph and neutral connectors between its components. Both the
weights and the acceptance threshold are fixed constants. A weighted palette forces every accepted
target coloring to respect the source lists, so restricting the target coloring recovers a source witness. The
construction has linear graph size. The resulting decision problem is NP-complete. The contribution is
this restricted endpoint: the palette and precoloring mechanisms themselves are established techniques.

1. Introduction
Coloring with bundles charges for the largest color used within each prescribed vertex set. Requiring
each set to induce a connected subgraph is a natural structural restriction. Ito et al. [1, Theorem 8] give
a polynomial-time algorithm for connected partitions on trees. Their bipartite hardness results with a
constant number of bundles [1, Theorems 10–11] do not require those bundles to be connected. This
leaves a specific question: can hardness persist with both connected bundles and a constant bundle
count?
This paper gives an affirmative answer for seven weighted bundles. The six singleton bundles force a
three-color palette by a numerical cost inequality. One connected bundle holds the original vertices and
auxiliary vertices that join source components without restricting their color choices. Our main result
includes recovery from every accepted target coloring.
Theorem 1. Let the target consist of a simple bipartite graph with an ordered partition into seven
nonempty connected bundles, the first six singleton. Fix the weights and threshold to
                       (𝑤1 , …, 𝑤7 ) = (4096, 1024, 256, 64, 16, 4, 1), 𝑇 = 5823.                         (1)

Deciding whether a proper positive-integer coloring has weighted sum of bundle maxima at most 𝑇 is
NP-complete. There is a deterministic polynomial-time Karp reduction from Bipartite List 3-Coloring,
with polynomial-time recovery of a source coloring from every accepted target coloring. On an 𝑛-vertex,
𝑚-edge source, its output has 𝑂(𝑛 + 𝑚 + 1) graph size and 𝑂((𝑛 + 𝑚 + 1) log(𝑛 + 2)) encoded bits.
The unrestricted number-of-bundles case already includes ordinary sum coloring through singleton
bundles. Our result concerns the constant-count restriction. It neither locates the smallest hard con­
nected-bundle count nor classifies the unweighted case. Section 5 compares the construction with older
palette and precoloring results.

4. The complete reduction rule
4.1. Forward construction
Validate the source schema and bipartiteness. For a valid nonempty source, order its components by
their smallest vertex. In each component, choose that vertex as representative and orient the component
bipartition so that the representative lies on side 𝐴. Keep every original vertex and edge. Between each
consecutive pair of representatives insert a fresh vertex on side 𝐵 adjacent to both. Put all these vertices
in 𝐵7 . If the source is empty, use one isolated dummy vertex on side 𝐴 as 𝐵7 instead.
Append the palette, with each 𝑎𝑗 on side 𝐴 and 𝑏𝑗 on side 𝐵. For each original vertex 𝑣 and each color
𝑗 ∉ 𝐿(𝑣), join 𝑣 to the opposite-side palette vertex of color 𝑗: to 𝑏𝑗 if 𝑣 is on side 𝐴, and to 𝑎𝑗 otherwise.

Assign original vertices the first 𝑛 indices, then the connectors or dummy, then the palette vertices in
their stated order. Sort the output edges. These choices specify a deterministic construction, denoted 𝑓.
Lemma 3 (legal output). Every constructed target is simple and bipartite, and its seven bundles form
a connected partition.
Proof. Original edges and palette edges are simple and bipartite. Each connector is fresh and joins
two distinct side-𝐴 representatives. Each list edge joins an original vertex to a distinct opposite-side
palette vertex; these edges cannot duplicate any other edge type. Every added edge crosses the declared
bipartition. The component chain connects the induced subgraph on 𝐵7 ; in the empty case that subgraph
has one vertex. The other bundles are nonempty singletons. They partition the output vertices.        □

4.2. Witness recovery and equivalence
Given the source and a target coloring, the recovery algorithm reconstructs 𝑓(𝐺, 𝐿) and checks the
coloring’s length, positive integer entries, edge constraints and weighted cost. It rejects an invalid
recovery input explicitly. For an accepted coloring, it returns the first 𝑛 colors.
Lemma 4 (both directions). A valid source has a list coloring if and only if its constructed target has
an accepted coloring. Every accepted target coloring recovers a valid source list coloring.
Proof. Given a source coloring, keep its colors on the original vertices. Each connector must avoid at
most two colors, so assign it the smallest remaining color in {1, 2, 3}. Give an empty-source dummy

color one and the palette coloring 𝑑. Source edges remain proper. Each list edge joins a source vertex to
a color absent from its list, so it is proper as well. The maximum on 𝐵7 is at most three; the total cost
is at most 5823.
Conversely, take any accepted target coloring. Apply Lemma 2 with the actual maximum on 𝐵7 . The
palette is fixed to 𝑑, and every original vertex has color in {1, 2, 3}. A list edge forbids exactly its
associated missing color. All original edges remain present. Restricting the coloring therefore yields a
proper source coloring respecting every list. The empty restriction is valid when 𝑛 = 0. This proves
both the NO implication and the recovery claim.                                                       □

4.3. Totality and resource bounds
For any malformed source string, output the construction for a single vertex with an empty list. This
target has seven vertices; its main vertex is adjacent to 𝑏1 , 𝑏2 , 𝑏3 . It is legal by Lemma 3 and is NO by
Lemma 2, since the main vertex has no available color. Thus malformed strings preserve the language
equivalence too.
The implementation reads explicit JSON graphs and lists. It disables Python’s decimal-integer digit
cap before decoding, then checks the count against the explicit list array before allocating the graph.
Invalid JSON and source-schema violations use the fixed NO output. This avoids a runtime-dependent
limit on the length of integer tokens. Decimal conversion and validation take polynomial time in the
string length.
For a nonempty source with 𝑘 components, the output vertex and edge counts satisfy
                                     |𝑉 (𝐻)| = 𝑛 + 𝑘 + 5 ≤ 2𝑛 + 5,                                      (6)

                           |𝐸(𝐻)| ≤ 𝑚 + 2(𝑘 − 1) + 3𝑛 + 6 ≤ 𝑚 + 5𝑛 + 4.                                 (7)

The empty-source and malformed-source outputs have constant size. Vertex identifiers require
𝑂(log(𝑛 + 2)) bits, and the numerical parameters are fixed, so the output encoding has 𝑂((𝑛 + 𝑚 +
1) log(𝑛 + 2)) bits.
Graph validation, component traversal, sorting and serialization all have polynomial worst-case cost in
the explicit source length, including the bit cost of reading integer tokens. The constructor performs
no coloring search. Recovery reconstructs the same target, checks the supplied witness and returns its
prefix; its running time is polynomial in source-plus-witness length.
Proof of Theorem 1. Lemmas 3 and 4, the fixed NO output and the resource bounds give the claimed
deterministic Karp reduction and recovery algorithm. The source is NP-hard by [2, Theorem 5]. For
target membership in NP, every accepted vertex color is at most 5823, since all weights are positive
and every vertex belongs to a bundle. A coloring thus has polynomial length, and graph legality,
connectivity, properness and cost can be checked in polynomial time.                              □

5. Relation to previous constructions
The mathematical techniques have older precursors. Bodlaender, Jansen and Woeginger [3, Theorem
1] prove hardness of three-color extension on bipartite graphs with a designated precolored vertex for
each color. Chlebík and Chlebíková [2, Theorem 6] use precolored neighbors to exclude missing list
colors. Murat and Paschos [4, Proposition 1] use the same off-diagonal six-vertex palette, with separated
probabilities, for a probabilistic coloring objective. We do not claim that graph or the list encoding
as new.
The endpoint here adds fixed weighted maxima over a partition whose seven parts all induce connected
subgraphs. The cost inequality forces the palette, and neutral connectors enforce connectivity without
changing source satisfiability. The cited precoloring results alone do not impose these two requirements.
The bundle constructions in [1, Section 4] permit disconnected parts; the tree algorithm in [1, Section
3.4] has a different graph domain.

A. Verification and reproducibility
Finite checks test the implementation; they do not replace the general proof. Two separately written
testing programs execute the candidate in fresh processes without importing it. The prepared source
oracle uses Z3 list constraints. The separate verifier enumerates source assignments and uses exact
nested maxima for target cost. Both validate actual target graphs and recovered witnesses.
The prepared suite covers ten cases with nine recoveries. A seeded suite covers 160 instances on at
most seven vertices, including 126 YES and 34 NO cases, with 347 recoveries. Its seed is 20260916. The
separate verifier covers all pairs of lists from ∅, {1}, {2}, {3}, {1, 2}, {1, 2, 3} on two vertices, with and
without an edge, plus empty and disconnected pinned cases: 74 decisions and 112 recoveries. These
counts are not deduplicated.
The prepared and separate suites passed again after a parser-only repair. Eight additional raw
malformed-input cases produce legal NO targets, including oversized integers. The seeded evidence
predates this repair and tests the unchanged graph construction. Independent review adds five decisions
and all 16 accepted target witnesses of those cases, plus eight parser regressions and one legal forward/
recovery check. No remaining defect was identified in that review; this is not a guarantee supplied by
finite testing.
Reproduction uses Python 3.14.7, NetworkX, Z3 and Typst as installed for the recorded runs. Python
must support sys.set_int_max_str_digits (available from 3.11). From the repository root, enter the
campaign:
